00 · The Set

Nine Invariants, Each With Its Check

The whole of IDUI as it ships in r32: nine rules, each enforced by a gate the deploy runs before production changes. The formal cards in 02 give each its concern, its question and its consequences; this is the short form a second project would start from. Rules that belong to this app and not to the method - Title Case hero titles, a skeleton on Today, no retried page transition - stay with Sindhorn and are not in the set.

The gates, by their short names: ratchet is ui-centralization-budget.mjs, page audit is page-centralization-audit.mjs, nested-glass is nested-glass-smoke.mjs, shape audit is ui-shape-source-audit.mjs, font-architecture is the typography test, invariants is idui-invariants-smoke.mjs (r32) and render smoke is ci-page-render-smoke.mjs. Section 06 lists them with what each refuses.

The foundation is seven files: app-tokens.css (values), app-glass.css (material), app-components.css (primitives), app-compositions.css (compositions, r32), app-shell.css (the frame), fonts.css (the face) and ci-library.css (the library page's own chrome). A composition is made of primitives and shaped by one page's content - the promotion card, the directory, the table, the business card; a primitive is vocabulary any page could use, whatever today's consumer count. When a second page adopts a composition it is not promoted; it is questioned. /ci labels each section by tier.

01 · Methodology

Small Rules, Strong Consequences

IDUI is a way of building an interface in which product decisions stay with the page, behavior lives in a small vocabulary of primitives, and everything visual is derived from a few orthogonal rules and one set of tokens. Its aim is the smallest number of independent design decisions needed to express the product correctly.

Why it exists

A refusal, not a philosophy

IDUI did not begin as an attempt to write a UI methodology. It began as a refusal to keep fixing CSS symptoms. The builder, a graphic designer without formal software-engineering training, kept meeting the same cycle from AI-assisted work: the agent sees a visual problem, adds CSS, the bug moves, another override lands, the old rule stays, the page looks right on the screen in front of it, and the architecture is worse than before. The dirt goes under the rug. The reaction was not to repair the pages more carefully but to repair the system that produces the pages.

Two instincts from that period became rules. The first is about instruction. "Make this page follow that page" is reference-driven: it asks the agent to copy an instance, and an instance can always be misread — spacing copied wrong, a context-specific class kept, an old bug inherited, a new local exception created. IDUI asks instead for the rule that produced both pages, and derives the new page from it. A rule is much harder to misinterpret than an example. The second is a dislike of fractions: 18px here and 20px there, 0.28 and 0.34, a variant that is almost another variant. Those are continuous local discretion, and every one is a decision that nobody meant to make. The method prefers a discrete space — one blur, two material weights, three control floors, eight type steps — so the architecture makes the decision once and pages cannot make it again.

The aim is the line the builder stated for it: if the architecture is clever enough to design itself, the job is done, and creativity can go anywhere it likes. For publication: the purpose of the architecture is to make routine interface decisions automatic, so that human attention is spent on product, composition, content and experience rather than on styling decisions that should never have been decisions. IDUI does not remove design. It removes the repeated decisions that were never design.

The problem it solves

Historical differences are not design concepts

The first Sindhorn Midtown Internal app grew fast: 1,167 commits in its first eight days. By 3 September 2026 it carried 30 route stylesheets, 715 !important declarations, 71 backdrop-filter rules outside the material file, 70 distinct font sizes, 36 distinct radii and 10 separate card implementations. Three routes had each named the same accent color under their own variable. The material every page used had been re-described, slightly differently, by every page.

An early step in the rebuild was to inspect and classify roughly two hundred historical card classes and a 61-name material registry. That work was stopped. The owner asked instead whether a much smaller set of rules could make most of those distinctions irrelevant. One question replaced the inventory: does this element draw an edge? If it does, it is a surface and takes the one material. If it only arranges its children, it takes nothing. If the question does not apply, the element is outside the rule. That way of thinking is the foundation of everything below.

Three concerns, kept apart

Composition

What exists on this page, and in what order?

Belongs to page code and is authored on purpose. Today has a hero, business metrics, F&B, rooms, outlook and notes; Jobs has filters, job records, status and actions; Brand has hotel facts and a business card. IDUI never generates information architecture.

A page author decides

Content and its meaning. Hierarchy, order and grouping. Domain semantics. Which behavioral primitive a thing needs. Semantic tone or state where it cannot be derived.

A page author never decides

Radius. Blur. Fill opacity. Typeface, size or weight. Border appearance. A color for a component. A spacing value. A control height. An animation. What a card looks like. Pages compose. Pages do not style.

Behavior

What does this thing actually do?

Belongs to a minimal vocabulary of primitives: button, field, selector, check, disclosure, dialog, toast, chip, navigation, code entry. Each exists because it has a genuinely different interaction contract, state machine, keyboard and focus behavior, accessibility semantics or lifecycle. The vocabulary is necessary; the goal is the smallest one that behavior justifies. Behavior earns vocabulary. Appearance does not.

Appearance

Given what it is, its state and where it sits, what does it look like?

Derived, never authored per page: semantic and contextual properties go in, invariant rules decide the treatment, tokens supply the values. A component may expose surface, tone, state, priority and hierarchy. It never exposes a styling knob.

Declaredata-tone="danger" — the reading is failing
Nevercolor:#E3A2A8 — a page choosing a red
Declare.app-overlay — this surface floats above content
Neverbackground:rgba(38,32,49,.72) — a page choosing a weight
Declarearia-pressed="true" — this chip is selected
Neverborder-width:2px — a page drawing selection

Changing class="warning-card" into data-tone="warning" is not by itself invariant-driven. Attributes, classes and tokens are mechanisms. What matters is where the decision originates: if page code still picks the look, the syntax changed and the architecture did not.

The fourth concern: validation

Validation

Is the resulting state legal?

An invariant that lives only in a document is weaker than one that can refuse an invalid result. Composition asks what belongs; behavior asks what it does; appearance asks what treatment follows; validation asks whether the state that results is allowed. It belongs to contracts: a declared condition, measured on the real output, with a consequence when it fails.

CompositionWhat belongs here? → pages
BehaviorWhat does it do? → primitives
AppearanceWhat treatment follows? → invariants and tokens
ValidationIs the resulting state legal? → contracts
Around all fourVariation explores within the valid space.

Rules define the valid design space. People and generators are free to explore inside it. The rules are hard; the result is not. This is what separates the method from a component library: a library offers approved parts, a contract refuses disallowed outcomes whatever parts produced them.

Invariant maturity

Level 1 · DocumentedA written rule"Glass must not nest." Followed by discipline; broken politely.
Level 2 · Machine-enforcedA check rejects violationsThe nested-glass smoke fails the build on a blurred element inside glass.
Level 3 · Adversarially exercisedThe system searches for violationsConditions that might break the rule are generated on purpose, and the rule must survive them. The Betta contract validator (05) is the one Sindhorn example.

Either, or, neither

The shape of a rule

Does this element draw a bounded foreground surface?

EitherSurfaceIt draws an edge, a border or a fill. It takes the canonical material.
OrStructureIt only arranges its children. It stays transparent and draws nothing.
NeitherOutsideText, an icon, a hairline, a chart line, a color swatch. The rule does nothing.

The third outcome is the important one. IDUI does not require every element to take part in every rule, and it refuses to invent a category just so that everything can be classified. Non-participation is a valid outcome. Where a domain genuinely has more than three states, the rule says so; the trinary shape is a preference, not a law.

Orthogonality

One rule, one concern

The interface is the intersection, not a component per intersection

Never write "a selected, critical, floating, interactive card looks like X". Separate the dimensions: surface? floating? nested? interactive? pressed? tone? disabled? Each invariant owns one, and the final treatment is what they produce together.

Worked example from production, the job status control (r23): it is a badge, so it takes the badge tint by tone; it is a selector, so it opens a listbox; it is compact, so it takes the inline height floor; it sits inside a card, so it drops the blur. Four rules meet and no new component exists. Its open menu is lifted to <body> because glass never nests — the library's one runtime geometry, never a page's.

InvariantDecides which treatment applies. A bounded surface → the on-page material weight.
TokenDecides the value of that treatment. --app-glass-fill, --app-glass-filter, --radius-surface, --control, --type-body.
PageKnows neither. It never writes .30, 18px, 14px or 13px, because those are system decisions.

What the method protects

Composition freedom

Invariant appearance must not become invariant composition

A factsheet, a job tracker, a digital business card and the Today dashboard have very different compositions and obey the same appearance laws. The system determines treatment, not product meaning. Over-correcting into pages that all look structurally identical is a failure of the method, not a success.

Complexity containment

Concentrate complexity where it creates value; eliminate it where it only creates choices

The Betta atmosphere is deliberately complex: a 533,943-byte WebGL runtime, shaders, an eight-period day cycle, satellite imagery as its only live input. That complexity is intrinsic and it is contained behind one canvas and one API. It justifies nothing in the card taxonomy, in spacing, in blur variants, in page materials or in transitions. Whenever complexity appears, ask whether it is intrinsic to the product or merely accumulated by the implementation. Preserve and contain the first; find the invariant that makes the second disappear.

Performance as architecture

A rendering constraint that changes what may exist is an invariant

"Glass never nests" is physics: backdrop-filter cannot sample past an ancestor that already has one, so nested glass renders as a flat tint whatever the CSS asks for. This was measured, not reasoned — an identical dropdown blurred on the F&B route and not on the library page, because the specimen container was itself glass. The transition ladder of 4 September (r13–r14e) is the same lesson from the other side: view-transition-name on the page host made it a backdrop root and flattened every card on a real phone, while headless Chrome barely showed it. Rules of this kind are first-class and are separated from design decisions: only the second kind is open to revision.

Working with an AI agent

Do not ask the agent to rediscover taste that is already encoded

The failure mode is familiar: inspect many historical variants, classify them, preserve the distinctions, generate more local decisions. The IDUI loop is different: read the invariants, understand the semantics, pick the behavior primitive, compose the page, derive the appearance, validate. The agent behaves like a compiler of product intent through design law rather than an independent visual designer.

The instruction follows the same shape. The agent is never told to make one page look like another; it is told which rule both pages obey, and a page that cannot be derived from the rules is a missing rule, not a missing reference. By default the agent invents nothing visual: no radius, blur, material, shadow, card type, type hierarchy, animation, control height, semantic color or spacing. When the existing invariants cannot express a result it stops, names the missing semantic requirement, tests whether an existing invariant is incomplete, runs the new-invariant test, and reports before introducing vocabulary. In this project that is written into AGENTS.md as "rules over inventories" and the inventory tripwire.

Workflow for migrating a legacy interface

  1. Inspect rendered outcomes. Start from what people see and do, never from class names.
  2. Separate behavior from appearance. Which differences are interaction, state or accessibility; which are styling and history.
  3. Identify invariants. Seek the smallest rules that explain the appearance. Run the simplification checkpoint before any table grows past a modest size.
  4. Preserve genuine behavior. Map distinct interactions to primitives.
  5. Delete appearance-only taxonomy. Keep a historical variant only while migration needs it, and say so.
  6. Move values into tokens. No page-level design literals.
  7. Move visual decisions into invariants. Pages stop controlling appearance.
  8. Compose with primitives. Page-specific content and hierarchy stay.
  9. Add enforcement. A rule that lives only in a document will be broken politely.
  10. Validate real output. Real phones, both themes, long content, nested surfaces, reduced motion, performance. A headless browser is evidence, not proof.

In Sindhorn this order is literal: the library page came first (3 September), the ratchet froze duplication the same day, the glass rule replaced the registry, and pages landed one at a time on top — Today, F&B, Brand, Messages, Settings, Jobs, Voice, Readability — each verified live before the next.

INV-MATERIAL-SURFACE

Implemented

Material membership

ConcernWhich elements receive the frosted material.
QuestionDoes this element draw an edge — a border or a fill?
EitherYes: it is a surface on the atmosphere. It takes the one material: fill, blur, edge.
OrNo, it only arranges its children: it is structure and takes nothing. A frameless utility action is structure.
NeitherText, icons, hairlines, focus rings, chart lines. A color swatch draws a fill but the fill is data, not a surface; it is outside the rule.
Consequence.app-card, .app-control and the components that are cards by definition (.app-surface, .app-action-card, .app-disclosure, .app-masthead, .app-navbar) get material from app-glass.css alone. A well — badge, avatar, field input — draws a fill, so it borrows the blur and keeps its own tokened tint.
InputsWhether the component declares an edge. Nothing about its page, route, department or content.
ForbiddenA page or component declaring background, border-color or backdrop-filter. An empty frame drawn "to have no background" — an unpainted edge is still an edge.
ExceptionsNone. This rule replaced a 61-name hand-maintained registry, and a rule-with-exceptions would be that registry again.
Enforcementci-page-render-smoke.mjs reads the computed material of every canonical selector (CARD, BARE, WELL, OVERLAY). page-centralization-audit.mjs rejects backdrop-filter anywhere but app-glass.css. The ratchet counts material rules outside the canonical file.
Specimens/ci §01 One Frosted Material, §05 Cards Communicate Role, §24 What Holds the System Together.

INV-MATERIAL-WEIGHT

Implemented

Two weights of one material

ConcernHow much pigment a surface carries.
QuestionDoes this surface float above page content rather than sit on the atmosphere?
EitherYes — a dialog, sheet, menu, toast, or a sticky table column over scrolling cells: overlay weight, --app-glass-overlay-fill (.72).
OrNo — a card, the masthead, the navbar: on-page weight, --app-glass-fill (.30), light enough for the Betta to show through.
NeitherNot a surface (see INV-MATERIAL-SURFACE).
ConsequenceSame blur either way. There is no third recipe for a surface. The overlay weight is never pushed past about .92; at that opacity it reads as solid black and stops being glass, which is how a legacy menu ended at .98.
InputsWhether the component is an overlay (.app-overlay) — a lifecycle fact, not a look.
ForbiddenA component choosing its own opacity. A "stronger" or "lighter" glass for one page.
ExceptionsNone.
EnforcementRender smoke: .app-overlay, .app-sheet, .app-toast must compute to OVERLAY; .app-table tbody th to STICKY.
Specimens§08 One Selector, One Menu; §10 One Dialog Standard; §21 Dense Data Scrolls; §16 A Transient Confirmation.

INV-MATERIAL-NESTING

Implemented

Glass never nests

ConcernRendering physics of backdrop-filter.
QuestionIs this material surface inside another material surface?
EitherYes: it keeps its tint and drops the blur. An overlay inside an overlay takes the near-solid nested tint (.98) because its tint alone must carry legibility.
OrNo: the normal blur applies.
NeitherNot a surface.
ConsequenceStructural selectors in app-glass.css (.app-card .app-control, .app-overlay .app-overlay …) remove the blur. A selector menu opened inside a card is lifted to <body> so it can blur at all. A card never contains a card; groups inside one card are .app-card-sections separated by a hairline.
InputsDOM ancestry only.
ForbiddenA route fighting the rule or giving a nested surface its own blur. Opacity below 1, a transform, or view-transition-name on any ancestor of frosted cards — each makes the ancestor a backdrop root (r14d, reverted).
ExceptionsNone. This is measured physics, not a design choice, and is not open to revision.
Enforcementnested-glass-smoke.mjs renders 13 routes and fails on any blurred element inside a glass surface.
Specimens§05, §08, §23 One Job, Where It Stands (the nested status control).

INV-TOKEN-LOCALITY

Implemented

Values live in two files

ConcernWhere a design value may be written.
QuestionIs this a design value — a color, size, radius, duration, opacity, blur?
EitherYes: it exists once, as a token in app-tokens.css or as the material in app-glass.css, and is referenced by name everywhere else.
OrNo: it is geometry or structure (a grid, a padding, a gap) and may be declared by the component in app-components.css, preferably through a space or radius token.
NeitherData rendered into markup: a swatch's SVG fill, an image's ratio.
ConsequenceEight type steps, three weights, zero tracking, six radii, seven spaces, three control heights, two durations and one easing. A page module contains no literal at all.
InputsThe kind of value.
ForbiddenAny style attribute or <style> block; a color or pixel literal in a template; a route stylesheet.
ExceptionsTwo #000 mask stops in app-components.css (a fade mask is geometry, not tone). The scrim's fill and 7px kernel became two tokens in app-glass.css, the 6px check radius became --radius-tight and the legacy fonts.css rules went, all in r32. In the library, none in a page; listed so they are not mistaken for permission.
Enforcementpage-centralization-audit.mjs (style attributes, style blocks, color literals, pixel values, undefined classes); the ratchet's distinct-font-size, distinct-radius and inline-style metrics, which may only fall (both at 0 since r32); idui-invariants-smoke.mjs refuses a min-height literal outside the tokens.

INV-TONE

Implemented

A state is a tone, never a color

ConcernSemantic color.
QuestionIs this element in a state the employee must notice?
Outcomessuccess · danger · error · loading · quiet — declared as data-tone; or no tone. The domain has five states, so the rule has five outcomes and does not pretend to three.
ConsequenceTwo status colors exist (--app-success, --app-danger) plus the accent and the ink. A tone changes what a component already draws — a label, a value, a badge tint — and never adds a decoration. A badge is never the only carrier of meaning.
InputsThe state, from data. A page says "tight deadline"; it does not say "red".
ForbiddenA color literal in a page; a new tone for one page; color as the sole signal.
ExceptionsNone.
EnforcementAudit rejects color literals in page modules. There is no check yet that the library's own colors are only tokens (see 06).

INV-MOTION

Implemented

Two durations, one easing

ConcernMotion.
QuestionIs this press feedback, or a component settling into a new state?
EitherPress feedback: --motion-fast 160ms.

INV-PAGE-PRESENTATION

Implemented

INV-READABILITY

Implemented

Ink clears the composite on every period

ConcernContrast of text over the live material.
QuestionDoes this ink role clear 4.5:1 over the glass composite on the darkest and lightest Betta period?
EitherYes: the role and the period may ship together.
OrNo: either the period's fish changes (Readability Test, seed-reproducible) or the role's token changes. Never a page-local color.
NeitherDecoration that carries no meaning.
Current factThe Readability Test (r29a) measures ink, muted and accent from the rendered frame, softened and composited with the glass fill. Ink and accent pass on every bundled period. Muted at .55 read 2.7–3.9:1 on all of them, and measured across 40 fish on production it passed 6 - none in a bright period - because translucent ink has a contrast ceiling (5.9:1 over black at .55; 4.5:1 only under a sky darker than 25% grey). The worst sample sits mid-screen, not at a tail edge (excluding the page gutters rescued 1 of 40), and the glass fill is not a lever for muted (it darkens text and ground alike). The new-invariant test

  1. Is it based on semantics or context rather than one page?
  2. Does it govern a real design consequence?
  3. Will it apply beyond one instance?
  4. Does it remove author discretion?
  5. Is it orthogonal to the existing rules?
  6. Can it be explained without listing pages or components?
  7. Can it be tested automatically?
  8. Does it create an exception to another rule?
  9. Would amending an existing rule be cleaner?
  10. Is it actually a product decision in disguise?

03 · Primitives

Behavior Earns Vocabulary

An audit of what actually behaves in the shell today, judged by the new-primitive test: what interaction, state machine, keyboard and focus behavior, accessibility semantics or lifecycle does it have that nothing else expresses? Where the only difference is appearance, it is not a primitive. No count is targeted.

Button

Keep · one

.app-primary and .app-utility-action · native <button> / <a> · a command · focus ring, press scale

One behavior, two priorities. Primary is framed at the control floor, utility is frameless at the inline floor; that is weight, not behavior. Name them as the two priorities of one primitive and stop counting two.

Chip

Keep

.app-chip · button with aria-pressed, or a jump link · a filter or a toggle

A pressed state that persists is a different state machine from a command. The rail (Open · Stuck · Done · All) and the transport (Previous · Play · Next · Live) are both chips.

Icon control

Merge naming

.app-back-control, .app-masthead-action, .app-masthead-account · one 36px square by token · back, close, or a two-state mode

Three class names for one behavior family already unified by --size-icon-control. The two-state masthead controls (data-mode) are the one genuine state; back and close are commands. Candidate for one name with a mode, once the masthead's nesting is confirmed structural (it is).

Field

Keep

.app-field · native input, textarea, select, search with clear · label, note, native validation

Native behavior; the library only frames it. The search clear is a field feature, not a primitive.

Range

Keep

.app-field input[type=range] · .app-field-head · label and value on one row

A number set by eye rather than typed, added in r37 for the Betta composition editor. It is a field, not a new control: the track is the same inset well an input draws, the accent marks the thing being moved, and the value reads back beside the label so it stays legible under a finger. The thumb is the icon control's rounded square at a smaller size - this system has no circles and no capsules. Reach for it only where the exact figure is secondary; anything typed, checked or chosen stays an input.

Code entry

Keep

.app-code + app-code.js · six wells · typing advances, backspace retreats, paste fills

A distinct state machine shared by sign-in and the library specimen. Behavior lives in one module.

Check

Keep

.app-check · native checkbox in a row · done / option modes

Native. The two modes differ in where the box sits, which is composition.

Selector

Keep

.app-select + app-select.js · role="listbox" / option, aria-expanded · menu lifted to body inside a card

Compact is appearance (data-compact), correctly not a second primitive. The legacy selectField / bindSelectField pair that the old /share runtime used - two implementations of one behavior - was deleted with Phase 7 (r30); the module now carries one selector.

Disclosure

Done

.app-disclosure · aria-expanded + data-open · one rhythm · the clamp toggle is the same machine

It was the one real behavioral duplicate. The toggle was written four times — brand-page.js, fnb-page.js, today.js, signin-page.js — plus the library's own and the clamp toggle in F&B. Identical contract; only the per-page persistence differed. r35 replaced all of them with app-disclosure.js: toggleDisclosure for a page with its own click handler, bindDisclosures with an onToggle callback for one without. Jobs became the sixth consumer without a seventh copy, and the panel now answers to data-open rather than to .app-disclosure, so a card whose foot carries a status selector and an action - and so cannot itself be a button - folds one paragraph through the same machine, with the chevron alone as the toggle.

Sortable list

Keep

[data-sortable] · data-dragging · press and hold · app-drag-sort.js

The shell's first drag gesture, added in r35 for the job list. Held 280ms with a 10px slop, so an earlier movement is a scroll and the lift never happens — which is how it stays clear of the shell's own pull-to-refresh. The module owns no appearance: it stamps data-dragging and the foundation decides what that looks like, which is the overlay weight, because a lifted card floats over the list rather than sitting on the atmosphere.

Dialog

Keep

.app-dialog + app-dialog.js · native <dialog>, showModal, backdrop click closes, returnValue · confirm variant

The modal lifecycle, once. dialogHead() is the shared head with the close control.

Sheet

Merge into dialog

.app-sheet · a <dialog> created inside fnb-page.js with the same lifecycle · bottom position, grip

Identical state machine to the dialog; differs by geometry. By the new-primitive test it is the dialog with data-kind="sheet", opened by openDialog, and the page-local opener goes away.

Toast

Keep

.app-toast + app-toast.js · transient, timed, one at a time

A lifecycle nothing else has. Never blocks, never asks.

Navigation

Keep

.app-navbar-button, masthead home · aria-current · two sets that push between app and settings

Route state, owned by shell.js. The set push is the one shell choreography and it is a token, not a page decision.

Pressable surface

Neither

.app-action-card · a card that is a link · press feedback

Not a primitive: the interactive dimension applied to a surface. It is listed so nobody adds "card types" for it.

State, skeleton, track, badge, table, list

Neither

display of state and data · no interaction contract

Structure and state display. They participate in the tone and material rules and have no behavior to earn vocabulary with.

View transition

Decide

app-view.js · retained as the §17 specimen only; the shell does not use it

A behavior with no consumer. Either the specimen documents a decision (why the shell cuts plainly) or the module retires. Owner's call.

.app-quiet-action

Deleted, r32

was an alias in app-glass.css · zero uses

An appearance-only name with no consumer; it went with the other glass aliases in r32.

Reading the audit

Twelve behaviors today, ten by name after the mergers, and none of it a target

The vocabulary is already small because appearance never earned a name in the rebuild. What remains is naming debt (button priorities, icon controls), one appearance-only split (sheet), one alias, and one genuine duplication of behavior (disclosure). Keep chip and button separate: a persistent pressed state is a different contract. Keep toast, dialog and selector separate: their lifecycles differ. Do not merge distinct behaviors to make the list look elegant.

The new-primitive test, for anything proposed later: what interaction cannot an existing primitive express; what state machine is genuinely different; what keyboard or focus behavior differs; what accessibility semantics differ; what lifecycle differs; is the difference merely appearance; could an existing primitive plus a semantic state express it? If the only difference is appearance, reject it.

05 · Case Study

How the Method Emerged

Factual, from the repository's own history and documents. The strength of the case is the evidence, not the builder.

25 August 2026 — the first architecture

The repository begins. The app is aligned to the Flipgazine CI (commit 4ca81fb) and given "sticky Liquid Glass chrome". A specialist document maps brand colors and a bilingual typography rule. Each route ships its own stylesheet.

25 August – 1 September — accumulation

1,167 commits in eight days: weather WebGL, Phase 6 push, Phase 8 sky, Phase 9 auth, a wet-glass refraction shader added and removed within a day. Every route re-declared the material under its own names; the accent lived as --fnb-accent, --fs-accent and --settings-accent. A "patch layer" convention appeared: -refinements, -fixes, -polish, -stability stylesheets on top of stylesheets.

Late August — centralizing mature code, and losing the race

A first from-scratch rebuild lost to the live app twice. Route CSS kept reinventing material under new names, and review by phone screenshot could not see that half of the library page's own components had no CSS behind them. The live app gained roughly 200 commits in five days while the rebuild stood still. An extraction attempt lifted 57 rules out of three route stylesheets by script and bolted route names on as co-selectors; it dropped resets, missed sub-elements, left components with no material, and each repair was a patch on a patch. These are the "unsuccessful, expensive attempts" of the origin story; their cost in hours is not recorded.

2 September — rebuild from centralized foundations

Eight requirements are fixed: no reinstall, centralized modules, no inline patches, no per-page CSS, real frosted glass everywhere, the Betta engine intact, fast launch, skeleton loading. The order is fixed too: library first, then rules, then pages one at a time.

3 September — the library, the ratchet and the rule

/ci is rebuilt as a standalone page. The centralization ratchet records the baseline (30 route sheets, 715 !important, 70 font sizes) and forbids any metric from rising. The glass membership rule replaces the 61-name registry with one question, after measuring on the library page itself that nested blur renders flat. The two-hundred-class inventory is abandoned.

4 September — Today, and a rule that was wrong

Today is rebuilt on the library (Phase 1). The transition ladder r13–r14e tries four ways to animate page changes; each either lagged or flattened the glass, and the View Transitions attempt made the page host a backdrop root on a real phone. It was reverted the same day to a plain swap. The lesson entered the rules: never put opacity, a transform or a transition name on an ancestor of glass.

5 September — pages on the canonical architecture

r15 to r29a: Settings, Broadcast, the shell cutover (legacy sources deleted, gates retuned), Jobs, the Voice copy library, Artwork copy, control heights, the Readability Test. "Rules over inventories" is written into AGENTS.md with the tripwire: if centralizing needs dozens of variants enumerated, stop and propose the rule.

5 September, later — the contract leaves the page

r29b moves the saved fish to the server: a table nobody can read or write directly, an anonymous read that returns styles and nothing else, and a save RPC that re-checks the 4.5:1 reading itself and refuses below it. r29c adds the release gate: every saved style must still be drawn by its seed under the code about to ship. The contract now holds at authoring, at the server and at release; the page is one of three places it is enforced, not the only one.

5 September, evening — the token was the ceiling

The owner could save the dark half of the day and none of the bright half, and asked whether light fin edges at the screen edge were the cause. Measuring instead of guessing: 40 fish on production, the worst sample mid-screen, the page gutters worth one fish, the glass worth nothing for muted - and the muted token itself unable to pass on any sky brighter than 25% grey. r29d raised --app-muted from .55 to .70 and nothing else; the owner then chose all eight fish in under an hour and locked them. The lesson the method keeps: when every candidate fails, suspect the rule's constant before the candidates.

5 September, night — the last of the old app leaves (Phase 7, r30)

The public /share pages had been the one place the old app still ran: eleven legacy files, 249 !important, a second Betta bootstrap and a second selector, all kept so a shared promotion would render for a guest. r30 replaces the pack with the shell itself in public mode - the same page module, the same five foundation files, one CSS rule (body[data-public]) - and deletes the pack, its test, its smoke scripts and its hotfix workflow. The ratchet baseline drops to the numbers above. In the same release the atmosphere gains its second mode: the sky, made from the saved fish's own colors, for reduced motion and for phones that cannot hold the fish - decided by measuring frames, not by guessing devices. The eight saved styles were not touched.

5 September, late night — the last route joins the shell (r31)

The public business card had been dead in production since 30 August: its page was a fourth document with its own three stylesheets and override sheet, and the function that was to serve it sat in a directory Cloudflare's advanced-mode worker never reads. r31 makes the card the shell in public mode, the way r30 made /share: the Pages worker cuts index.html per request with the same transformation the share generator runs (public-page.js, one module for both), stamps the card's own title and Open Graph tags and its data as a bootstrap script, and answers /<slug>.vcf from the same vCard builder Settings uses. The card body is one module (business-card-page.js) that Settings › Me, its presenting dialog and the public page all render; the slug, QR and vCard mechanisms were verified and kept unchanged. Nine files deleted, among them app-shapes.css - the last stylesheet outside the foundation. The ratchet's "all" column now equals its "shell" column.

Result

The shell loads five foundation files and no route CSS; no stylesheet exists outside the foundation. Twelve page modules contain no design literal. One material, two weights, one blur, three control floors, eight type steps, two durations. Twelve behaviors.

Remaining debt

Three disclosure toggles and a sheet opener in page code. app-view.js without a consumer. Control-height and action-weight rules with no check. The /share pack and its selector pair, the muted-ink ceiling and the business card's own documents are gone (r29d, r30, r31).

Lessons

Measure, don't reason — nested glass and the transition ladder were both settled by a device, not an argument. A ratchet beats a rule the code cannot yet satisfy. Land one page at a time, verified live. A rule can be vetoed; a list cannot. The specimen page must render the real material or it documents a fiction. Inventories are evidence to inspect, never architecture to preserve.

The clearest specimen: the Betta contract validator

What the tool does · r29a, 5 September 2026

A generative atmosphere, a fixed contract

The Readability Test (#readability) is where each of the eight periods of the hotel day gets its Betta. For a period it shows a candidate rendered live behind the real interface, with its six-hex-digit seed, its palette swatches, and four actions: Random, Show, Save, Original. Below the candidate are three readings — Ink, Muted, Accent — taken from the rendered frame, softened as the glass softens it, composited with the on-page glass fill, and expressed as WCAG contrast. Each reading is the lowest the glass has shown since the period came on screen. A transport steps through periods, plays the day, or follows live hotel time. Under the candidate, a sample of real Today content is rendered on the same atmosphere.

Save is unavailable until all three readings clear 4.5:1. There is no aesthetic argument afterward: a candidate that fails is invalid, whatever it looks like.

PeriodWhich kinds of expression are appropriate now — the aesthetic envelope of that hour.
SeedWhich expression within that envelope. Deterministic: the same seed reproduces the same fish.
Generatorbetta-random.js, the Mac lab randomizer ported verbatim. Colors and fin form only; camera and composition are never randomized.

JudgmentAmong valid candidates, the designer chooses. The contract constrains the space; it does not pick inside it.

Why it matters to the method

Variation is made safe, not removed

The input space is large: eight periods, a fish morph, a seed, a palette, satellite imagery as the only live modulation, procedural movement. The contract stays fixed: material, typography, surface rules, nesting, motion, the contrast floor. The atmospheres in one session run through crimson, orange, blue, gold, violet — and the interface is recognizably one system in every one of them. The invariants do not make everything identical; they constrain the relationships that must stay true while the picture changes. That is the answer to the objection that strict invariants make pages rigid.

The worst frame, not a screenshot

A palette can pass on a still and fail five seconds later when a bright fin drifts under muted text. Because the reading is a running minimum over the period's time on screen, the validator judges the worst observed interaction between a moving atmosphere and a stationary surface. This is materially stronger than checking a static palette, and it is what the Readability Test does that a palette table cannot.

Random is design-space sampling

Rather than composing eight palettes by hand and hoping they hold, the tool generates candidates and makes each prove itself against the contract. Generate → render through the production system → observe over time → measure → keep or reject. The nearest analogy in software is property-based testing: declare a property that must always hold, generate inputs, and try to find one that breaks it. Here the property is readability and the inputs are atmospheres. The analogy is an interpretation, not a claim of novelty.

Two validation layers

The formal layer rejects objectively invalid candidates. The perceptual layer — real production composition on the real atmosphere — lets the designer reject a valid candidate that is unpleasant. Both stay. Invariants constrain the valid design space; they do not replace design judgment inside it.

What the contract already found

The bundled presets predate the contract. Measured against it, muted ink fails on every one of the eight periods (2.7–3.9:1) while ink and accent pass. The contract rejected the production atmosphere before any opinion about it had been offered, and the resolution went through the token rather than the fish: the failing reading was the ceiling of a translucent muted ink, raised to .70 in r29d, after which all eight periods were chosen against the contract and locked on 5 September. That sequence is the best evidence in the project that a contract outranks taste.

Positioning

Invariant validation harness

The methodology-level name for this class of tool. In Sindhorn it is the Betta contract validator. The principle generalizes to whatever varies: responsive widths, long translated strings, extreme data values, dynamic imagery, focus order, animation budgets — generate the variation and prove the invariants survive it. Only the Sindhorn instance is observed; the generalization is a hypothesis (07). Its limits are also plain: the measurement itself is driven by a person at authoring time and runs on real WebGL, where a headless gate would be brittle; what release CI checks (r29c) is that every saved seed still draws the fish that was measured, not the reading itself.

6 · A second harness · hypothesized

The same shape as the Betta validator applied to content: generate Thai and English strings at the long end, counts and money at the extreme end, render the library specimens, and assert that no control drops below its floor and no surface gains a nested blur. Brittle or counterproductive

  • A headless contrast gate over live WebGL. Headless Chrome already disagrees with real phones on glass; a gate on its pixels would fail for the wrong reasons.

  • Screenshot diffs of whole routes. The Betta is live; every frame differs. Computed-style assertions, which the render smoke already uses, are the stable form.

07 · Limits and Positioning

What This Is, and Is Not

IDUI did not invent design systems, design grammar, model-driven UI, algorithmic layout, semantic tokens, generative UI, property-based testing or generative design. It proposes a practical architecture in which a minimal behavioral vocabulary is combined with small, orthogonal, machine-enforceable invariants so that appearance is derived rather than repeatedly authored. Sindhorn is a production case study, not proof of universal superiority.

Observed

In Sindhorn

The ratchet numbers. Zero page literals across eleven modules. One duplicated behavior. Two rules settled by measurement. Pages landing one a day once the library existed. A contract that refuses to save the current atmosphere's muted ink.

Inferred

Explained by the method

That a membership test outlives a registry; that a ratchet lets migration finish; that an agent given rules stops generating local decisions; that random sampling against a contract is property-based testing applied to appearance.

Hypothesized

To test elsewhere

That the four-layer split and either/or/neither shape hold in an unrelated product, with a different material, on desktop, in a light theme, with a larger team; that the validation harness generalizes beyond contrast. First test in §08: the material, scale and face held under a second identity; seven findings against the core. Second test, same day: one real Flipgazine page with its own engines, rebuilt with no CSS in the page; nine core assumptions moved to the constitutions.

Limitations

  • Behavior necessarily requires a catalog; IDUI shrinks it and does not remove it.
  • Not every design question is honestly binary or trinary; either/or/neither is a useful shape, not a universal law.
  • A poorly chosen invariant creates rigidity, and too many invariants recreate taxonomy in another form.
  • Semantic information must still be authored; tone, state and hierarchy are decisions a person makes.
  • Composition stays a human and product responsibility.
  • The evidence comes from one production system, one material, one dark theme, one phone-first product, one team of one plus an agent. The transfer in §08 is a second identity on the same dark, glass, phone-first premise, not a second product; the rebuild test that followed is one page of a second product, still dark and still glass.
  • A headless browser can pass every gate here and still differ from a phone; real devices remain part of validation.

Success condition

Can it handle a page the library has never shown?

A developer or an agent receives a requirement for an unseen page and: determines the composition; selects existing primitives; declares the necessary tone and state; writes no page-specific appearance; obtains a UI consistent with the whole product; passes the automated checks; adds a primitive only for new behavior; adds an invariant only where the existing laws cannot express a reusable consequence. If that holds, the system is invariant-driven. If a catalog example is needed for every situation, it is still example-driven. The Readability Test — a page with no precedent, built from the rules in one release with one new swatch primitive that the material rule already explained — is the closest evidence so far.

Findings against the core

T1 · Tracking is declared, not consumed

--tracking reaches no rule

Flipgazine's labels are tracked +0.16em; under the core they render at normal, the kicker and eyebrow included. Sindhorn holds the token at 0, so consuming it on the uppercase labels (kicker, eyebrow, surface label, metric label, table head) changes nothing here and gives a constitution the knob the README promises.

T2 · Rhythm is a literal

--space-* is unconsumed; gaps are written as pixels

Seven space tokens exist and no component reads one. A constitution cannot change the rhythm of a stack or a row. Fold the tokens or consume them; either is honest, the current state is neither.

T3 · The ground is not constitutional

#2E273B is a literal in app-shell.css

Both pages measure the same purple on body and the stage, beneath a green atmosphere in Flipgazine's case. Where the atmosphere does not paint, Sindhorn shows. One token, --app-ground, moves it to the constitution.

T4 · Controls fall to the UA's weight

Inputs and icon-only controls resolve font-weight: 400

The core sets the family on form controls but not the weight, so they take the browser's 400. Invisible under Sindhorn, whose regular is 400; visible under Flipgazine, whose regular is 300: a typed value sits heavier than its label. font-weight: inherit on .app-field input and .app-control.

T5 · Three weights, one shape

The core carries opinions beyond material

Flipgazine's 600 wordmark and 700 cover have no slot (the masthead mark rendered at 300) and its pill buttons are refused: .app-primary takes --radius-inset, and --radius-pill reaches only the badge. Both are the law, not a gap. "One face" says three weights and "rounded, never circular" says no pill on a control; a constitution accepts them or is not one. The README should say so.

T6 · A face must travel

Sindhorn's fonts.css points at /assets/fonts/, which the core does not carry

The smoke had to map the path to site/. A constitution is self-contained only if its files sit beside it and its URLs are relative. Flipgazine's imports from Google Fonts, which is a network dependency this app's own rules (offline shell, no reinstall) would refuse; fine for a test, not for a product.

T7 · Semantic slots demand values

--app-success and --app-danger had to be invented

Flipgazine defines no status tones. The core's badge, state and delta need them, so the constitution made them up. This is the right place for the invention, but the README should name the six slots a brand must fill and what each is used for.

The rebuild test: a real page, its engines carried

The specimen test above was self-confirming: IDUI markup rendered under a second set of tokens. The same day the method met its first external adversarial case. 09 · Architecture

The Shape, Provisional After Tests 01 And 02

Everything above describes rules and how they are enforced. This describes what the system is — the layers, which of them may decide what, and which direction they may depend in. It is stated now because it is finally derived from transfer evidence rather than from theory: Flipgazine removed the obvious Sindhorn assumptions, and Origarium exposed the deeper ones — material scope, a motion prohibition that was really a material one, text containment, and the gap between a rendered value and a design intent.

The core defines how design decisions are governed. The constitution defines which design decisions a product permits. The product composes meaning. None of them may quietly assume the taste of another.

v0.1, provisional. Test 03 and the tests still on hold can falsify any of this. What is frozen here is the shape of the system, not every field inside it.

Who may decide what

The cleanest boundary the method has: composition has semantic authority, not appearance authority.

LayerMay decideMay not decide
ConstitutionEvery value, and the relationships between them; what a material is; what a context meansWhich page renders what; anything naming an instance
CoreHow a rule resolves; what a primitive must do; what a contract enforcesAny value a constitution could want to change
Page or sceneWhat content exists, its hierarchy, grouping, ordering, semantic role, product state, which legitimate behaviour occursColour, blur, radius, font size, spacing, shadow, material, any local visual exception
Product engineIntrinsic complexity — procedural graphics, transitions, game mechanics, document interaction, domain logicPresentation on library elements; it is not an escape hatch

The engine layer is named deliberately. The Betta renderer, an e-ink bounce, an atmospheric engine — pretending specialised software is not specialised is how a small core cheats. D-B can be large and healthy; D-P attracts scrutiny. An engine's appearance parameters still derive from the constitution where they legitimately can.

Dependency direction

Stated as rules rather than a folder diagram, because rules can become checks.

CORE          must not import a product
              must not import a named constitution
              must not contain product identifiers

CONSTITUTION  may reference generic core concepts
              may scope by semantic context
              must not scope by page, route or instance
              must not contain screenshot coordinates

PAGE / SCENE  may reference semantic roles
              may use admitted behavioural primitives
              must not contain appearance literals

ENGINE        may implement intrinsic behaviour
              must not set presentation on library elements

The constitution rule is worded precisely because a blunter version would fail this system's own best work: Origarium's constitution legitimately contains [data-context="reader"]. Scoping by meaning is what a constitution is for. Scoping by instance is page CSS wearing a costume.

Core neutrality, and where it is currently violated

The boundary in one line: the core contains mechanisms, never a product's taste. So the core may know that materials have scope, how contextual rules resolve, how a primitive declares state, how text is contained, how contracts execute. It may not know glass, ivory paper, acid lime, a particular radius, a particular face, a hotel card or a paper thumbnail.

Measured against the shipped core today, that is not yet true. Published because an architecture whose first claim is aspirational should say so:

The word glass — 55 occurrencesA sheet named app-glass.css and a --app-glass-* token family. Since r41 the mechanism is generic — Origarium sets --app-document-filter:none and gets no blur — but the vocabulary is one product's material, and vocabulary is how taste survives a purge. Rename to a neutral family, or amend the rule to say the core may name a family it fully parameterises. Deferred until after Test 03: it touches every constitution and two gates
A product motif in the core — 7 classes, 14 occurrences.app-business-card-* in app-compositions.css, including .app-business-card-hotel. This is a named product artefact living where mechanisms belong
Product names in comments — kept"Sindhorn's surfaces are glass, so Sindhorn may not scale" is the record of why a mechanism is conditional. Classified evidence-only. Deleting it would make the core look neutral and be less honest

The test that decides it — proposed

Could the core produce a recognisable aesthetic with no constitution? Ideally not. If bare core already looks like Sindhorn, or like any general-purpose design system, something leaked.

The naive form of that test is weak: with no constitution every var() is unresolved and the page collapses to browser defaults, which proves only that the tokens live in constitutions — something the completeness contract already proves. The strong form is a deliberately null constitution: one grey, one face, no blur, one radius, all relationships intact. Then ask whether anything recognisable survives. That is falsifiable, and it doubles as the baseline for the contamination audit above.

Two invariants this architecture adds

Relocation is not centralisationMoving a local visual decision from a page into a product-specific constitution key does not centralise it; it only moves it. A key like papers-card-7-top-offset is page CSS in a costume, and without this rule a score of zero page CSS becomes meaningless. Partly checkable — a name carrying instance identity can be caught, and single-consumer tokens can be reported; the rest is review, and the doc says so rather than implying a gate that does not exist
A contract tests the applied state, never the written textFound the hard way in r50: the completeness contract passed for six releases while four --app-document-* declarations sat outside any rule in two constitutions, where the parser drops them. Scanning for a token proves it was written, not that it can be read. The contract now walks the braces, and the same shape — a rule that passes by never testing what it claims — is what the audit-coverage gap was too

Design intent is an input

The top of the diagram is the layer this method cannot generate for itself, and Test 02 is the proof: two reconstructions passed every contract, and what separated them was one sentence of intent that no instrument on the page could read.

Rendered outcomes expose values. They do not necessarily expose reasons. A constitution cannot reliably be reconstructed from rendered output alone, because some invariants encode design intent rather than observable appearance.

Which bounds every claim the architecture can make: centralising the wrong interpretation only makes the wrong interpretation systematic. Contracts enforce whatever understanding they were given, faithfully, everywhere. Where intent is unavailable — a third-party product — it must be inferred and labelled as inferred before reconstruction, so that a fidelity failure can be told apart from a governance failure. Test 02 records the finding; the Apple protocol carries it as an amendment.

Frozen, and not frozen

Frozen now

The layers. The dependency direction. The authority boundaries. Core neutrality as a rule. The D-B / D-P distinction. How validation relates to the whole. And the two cross-test laws the evidence produced: presentation burden counts only against an editorially accepted reconstruction, and evidence is append-only by experimental state.

Not frozen

The constitution schema. The complete primitive vocabulary. Any renderer or compiler interface. The final material model. Test 03 may still expose a missing distinction, and a generative test would stress this in a different direction entirely.
