01 · Voice

Where It Comes From

Sindhorn Midtown is a Vignette Collection hotel. Vignette speaks in short, warm, confident sentences about one-of-a-kind places and the people who run them. This app is that hotel talking to its own people, so it keeps the voice and drops the sales.

Vignette Collectionread on ihg.com, September 2026

What the brand does

Short declaratives. Second person, “you”, and a hotel that says “we”. No exclamation marks. American English. Oxford comma. One idea per sentence. Its words are independent-spirited, one-of-a-kind, thoughtful, character, story, memorable — and it names a property “Sindhorn Midtown Hotel Bangkok, Vignette Collection”, never “by IHG” in running copy.
Sindhorn Midtownsindhornmidtown.com

What the hotel does

Title Case headings (“Rooms & Suites”, “Restaurants & Bars”), sentence-case links, day-month-year dates (“31 January 2027”), lowercase am and pm (“11:30 am”, “6 pm – midnight”), prices as “THB 3,600++” and “THB 1,300 Net”. The Thai site keeps the 24-hour clock (“11:30 น. - 17:00 น.”) and the Buddhist year. This page settles the inconsistencies the site still carries.
This appthe hotel, to its people

What we do

The same warmth and the same confidence, spent on being useful. A control says what it does. A message says what happened and, if something went wrong, what to do next. Nothing is sold, nothing apologizes, nothing shouts. Guest-facing surfaces (16) get the full brand voice; everything else is the hotel being plain with a colleague.

02 · Registers

Who Is Reading

Who is reading decides the register. One voice; the volume and the vocabulary follow the reader.

StaffEvery signed-in page: Today, F&B, Jobs, Brand, Messages, Settings. Plain, warm, brief. “You” is the employee; “we” and “the hotel” are Sindhorn Midtown. Facts first, feelings rarely, and never a pitch.
GuestThe public share page and the business card (Phase 7). Full Vignette voice: a little more room, a little more story, the hotel's full name. Still no exclamation marks, still American English, still the same dates and times.
SourcedText the hotel or IHG already published — F&B promotion copy (17), the IHG history — is quoted as written, in whatever spelling it came with. Only a typo or a grammatical slip is corrected, in the data, with approval.
ArtworkThe artwork copy of a promotion (17): what the designer sets on a menu, a standee or a screen. The title is sourced, word for word. The subtitle and the body are the record read back in the staff register — the summary, then dates, outlets, hours, prices and the reservation line in one paragraph, the way this page writes them — with the press release beside it as the reference.

03 · Principles

Seven Habits

Lead with the thing.“No jobs yet”, not “It looks like you don't have any jobs yet.” The subject of the sentence is the subject of the screen.
Say what happens.A button names its result (“Publish”, “Revoke access”, “Save code and open app”). A toast confirms it in the past tense (“Published to All employees”). The two match.
Speak to one person.“You” is the employee holding the phone. “We” is the hotel. Never “the user”, never “users”.
Warm, not chatty.Contractions are welcome (“can't”, “didn't”, “you'll”). Jokes, emoji, “oops”, “awesome” and “please” are not. Vignette is warm because it is confident, not because it is cute.
One idea per sentence.Short declaratives. If a sentence needs a semicolon, it is two sentences. If a paragraph needs a third sentence, it is probably a list.
Explain, don't apologize.An error says what didn't happen and what to do: “Couldn't load your jobs. Check the connection and try again.” No “sorry”, no “unfortunately”, no transport internals.
Specific beats clever.“Saved on this phone · 5 Sep 2026 · 8:30 am” says more than “You're all caught up”. A number, a name or a time earns its place; a flourish does not.

04 · Language

American English, Always

Vignette Collection writes in American English and so does every word this app owns — copy, code comments, the UI Library's notes. The spellings below are the ones that kept slipping in.

Writecolor, center, centered, behavior, gray, organize, customize, canceled, catalog, program, favorite, license, analyze, traveled, toward, among, while, elevator, check, uncheck, inquiry, theater, cell or mobile, apartment, parking
Notcolour, centre, centred, behaviour, grey, organise, customise, cancelled, catalogue, programme, favourite, licence, analyse, travelled, towards, amongst, whilst, lift, tick, untick, enquiry, theatre, flat, car park
Keep as publishedProper names and sourced text: “Vignette Collection”, “IHG Hotels & Resorts”, “The Lobby Lounge”, the IHG history (“Our history” is IHG plc's own page) and F&B promotion copy. A name is spelled the way its owner spells it.
Employee IDTwo words, capital I, capital D. Not “employee number”, “staff ID” or “ID number”. The field on the sign-in page and in Admin says exactly this.

08 · Punctuation

The Marks

Few marks, used the same way. The typeface has zero tracking; the copy has zero decoration.

PeriodEnds a sentence in copy, a lede, a note, a dialog body, a state's copy line. Never on a title, a label, a button, a badge, a toast, a kicker, a list-row title or a state's title. “Saved”, not “Saved.”
ExclamationNever.
QuestionOnly when the copy really asks: a confirmation title (“Revoke access for Decha?”) or a help kicker (“First time here?”).
Middle dot ·Separates items in one line of metadata, spaced on both sides: “Operations · 5 Sep 2026 · 8:30 am”, “Access revoked · 2 sessions ended”. Not between clauses, not in a sentence.
Em dash —An aside inside a sentence, spaced on both sides in this typeface: “a done job's title steps back — nothing else changes”. At most one per sentence.
En dash –A range, unspaced: “6–11 pm”, “5–6 pm”, “September–October 2026”, “06:30–11:00” in data. Not a hyphen.
Hyphen -Compounds only: one-time code, 6-digit, 24-hour, on-the-books, day-to-night. Not for ranges, not as a dash.
Ellipsis …One character, only for a working state: “Signing you in…”, “Checking one-time code…”. Never in finished copy.
CommaOxford comma in every list of three or more: “weather, air-quality, business, and broadcast alerts”. No comma after a weekday in a date.
Quotes “ ”Curly in copy; straight only inside code. A title quoted in a dialog: Revoke “Lobby elevators serviced Tuesday morning”?
Apostrophe 'The keyboard mark, the same one every string in the code carries. Contractions and possessives are welcome: “can't”, “didn't”, “today's report”.
Ampersand &Only inside a name that owns one (F&B, People & Culture, Sip & Co., Rooms & Suites). In a sentence, “and”.
Slash /Only in a proper label that has one (“Rooms / Revenue”, “Audio/visual”). “Or” in a sentence.
ParenthesesRare. A unit or a clarifier: “(sqm)”. Never a whole sentence.
ColonIntroduces a list or a value in a label: “Deadline: none”. Not before a sentence.
SymbolsMinus sign − (U+2212) for a negative, never a hyphen. Percent unspaced “72.4%”. Degrees unspaced. Multiplication ×.

Relative“today”, “tomorrow”, “in 3 days”, “overdue” — lowercase after a middle dot (“Mon 8 Sep 2026 · today”), capitalized as a badge (“Starts tomorrow”). Never “yesterday at 6 pm”; a stamp is a stamp.
ZoneNever shown. Everything is hotel time. “ICT”, “GMT+7” and “Bangkok time” do not appear.
NumbersThousands separators from 1,000. Tabular figures in a column. Spell out one to nine in prose (“five failed attempts”), digits for everything with a unit (“6-digit code”, “15 minutes”, “3 sessions”). Units unspaced with symbols (72.4%, 172.5 sqm has a space because sqm is a word).

PhoneInternational, spaced in groups: “+66 2 796 8888”. A form's help says “such as +66…”.

12 · Feedback

A toast confirms; a badge states; a status line explains. None of them ends with a period, and none of them cheers.

Money“1,300 บาท”, “1,300 บาทสุทธิ”, “250++ บาท” — the amount, then the unit, then the condition, as the hotel's Thai site writes it.
NamesBrand and outlet names stay in Latin script: ANJU, Vignette Collection, IHG, Sip & Co., Bangkok'78. The hotel is “โรงแรมสินธร มิดทาวน์” in prose and “Sindhorn Midtown” in a label.

MixingA Thai sentence may hold an English name or a code (“Employee ID”, “ANJU”); an English sentence never holds Thai. Chrome never mixes: a label is one language.
Missing halfIf only one language exists, show it alone. Never machine-translate, never “(no Thai text)”.

17 · Promotion Copy

As Written by F&B

The English and Thai copy of a promotion is the F&B team's, approved with the artwork. The app renders it verbatim and the voice stops at its edge — then picks up again in the artwork copy, where the same record is read back for the designer.

Verbatim.Title, copy, terms, prices and dates appear exactly as provided. The app does not retitle, shorten, recase or rephrase them, and never adds a period, a dash or a badge inside them.
Typos and grammar only.A spelling slip or a broken sentence may be corrected. A word choice, a British spelling the team chose, a “Net” with a capital N, a “++” — these stay. When in doubt, it stays.
Fixed in the data, with approval.A correction is a change to the promotion record, proposed to the user with the before and after, and written only once approved. Never patched in the page.
The frame follows the voice.Everything around the copy — the status badge, “Starts tomorrow”, “Updated 5 September 2026 · 6 pm”, “Artwork done”, “Link copied”, the empty and error states — is the app's and follows this page.
Both languages, as given.The Thai and English halves are shown as written, the preferred one first. A half that was not provided is simply absent.
Artwork copy is read from the record, never stored.Section 03 of a promotion is what the artwork says. The title is the record's, verbatim. The subtitle is the press headline with its venue clause taken off — unless that is the title said again (“A Negroni Week Celebration”), in which case the next line or sentence that is neither the title nor a fact. The body is one paragraph, never a table: the promotion's summary and the press release's own sentences until the description has some weight, then the dates, outlets and hours, the prices, the terms, IHG One Rewards, the reservation line and the enrollment link, sentence after sentence, each written by app-format.js. Nothing is retyped: fix the record and the artwork copy follows.
The subtitle never repeats the title.“Father's Day” leads with “Come 3, Pay for 2”, not “Celebrate Father's Day with a Thai Feast”. A line may borrow a word from the title; it may not be the title over again.
The subtitle keeps the writer's case.“Taste the SPIRIT OF SEOUL, SOUL OF SIAM” stays as F&B shouted it; only a trailing period or exclamation mark is dropped. Recasing a headline is a rewrite, and a rewrite is the designer's call on the artwork.

What Holds the Voice Together

American English, always.Copy, comments and library notes. Sourced text and proper names keep their owner's spelling.
One word per thing.The vocabulary in 05 is the whole list. A new thing gets its word here before it gets a screen.
Title Case for a hero and a section title; sentence case for everything else.Two exceptions only. A button, a badge, a label, a toast and a row title are sentences.
A period ends copy, never a fragment.Titles, labels, buttons, badges, toasts, kickers and state titles carry no period. Copy lines, ledes, notes, dialog bodies and help do.
No exclamation marks, no “please”, no apologies.Warmth is confidence, not decoration. Thai “กรุณา” is the ordinary imperative and stays.
A button is a verb; a toast is its past tense.“Publish” → “Published to All employees”. “Revoke access” → “Access revoked · 2 sessions ended”.
An error is “Couldn't” + the thing, then the way out.Never the transport, never the code, never “unauthorized”. A gate says who it is for and who to ask.
Dates and numbers come from app-format.js.Day month year, four-digit year, no zone. English 12-hour with lowercase am and pm and no leading zero; Thai 24-hour with “น.” and the Buddhist year. No page formats a date of its own.
Metadata joins with a middle dot.“Operations · 5 Sep 2026 · 8:30 am”. A sentence uses commas and periods; a line of facts uses dots.
The staff register is plain; the guest register is the brand.Story, ritual and one-of-a-kind belong to the share page and the business card. Inside the hotel, the fact is the sentence.
Promotion copy is verbatim; artwork copy is derived.Typo and grammar fixes only, in the data, with approval. The frame around the copy, and the artwork copy read from the record, follow this page.
Copy is not inline.Strings live in the page module that owns them or in the data they describe, never in a style, a script fragment or a patch. A string that appears on two pages is written once.
